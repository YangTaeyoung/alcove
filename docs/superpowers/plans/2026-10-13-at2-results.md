### Display scale

Measured on this guest with `SystemParametersInfo` and
`DisplayConfigGetDeviceInfo` (type -3: lowest, current and highest step of a
display, counted from its recommended scale):

- `SPI_GETLOGICALDPIOVERRIDE` (0x009E) answered 0 whatever step was in force
  (0, 1, 2, -1); it does not report the current step.
- `SPI_SETLOGICALDPIOVERRIDE` (0x009F) stores the step as given
  (`HKCU\Control Panel\Desktop\PerMonitorSettings\<monitor>\DpiValue`, and the
  current step of `DisplayConfigGetDeviceInfo`) and returns true also for a
  step outside the offered range. Windows applies the step limited to that
  range: at 1280x800 (lowest 0, highest 1) step 1 gave a system DPI of 120,
  step 2 also 120, step -1 gave 96; at 1920x1080 (highest 3) step 2 gave 144
  and step 5 gave 168. A stored step above the range takes effect when the
  range grows: step 2 stored at 1280x800 became 150 % after `vm.set_resolution`
  1920x1080.
- A process started right after the call reads the new system DPI. The
  process that made the call keeps reporting the old one.
- The highest step was 1 at 1280x800, 3 at 1920x1080, 4 at 1920x1200 and 8 at
  3840x2160. The lowest step was 0 at each of them: the recommended scale of
  this guest is 100 % at every resolution. The engine's EDID describes a
  100 DPI display (`hw/display/edid-generate.c`: no physical size is passed,
  so one is derived from 100 DPI), so no state of this guest has another
  recommendation.

`dpiScale` therefore takes the recommended index from the lowest step (its
negative), sets `requested index - recommended index`, reads the system DPI
in a new process and reports that: `scale: 125 %` for a request of 125,
`ok: false` with `scale: 125 % was applied, not 150 %; the largest scale the
guest offers at its resolution is 125 %; ...` for 150 at 1280x800, where it
also stores the applied step so that a later resolution change keeps 125 %.
That the lowest step is the negative of the recommended index is confirmed
here only for a recommendation of 100 %.

### The engine abort in `snapshot-save`

`Error: ret = HV_ERROR (0xfae94001, at ../qemu-src/accel/hvf/hvf-all.c:77)` is
`hv_vm_protect(0x04000000, 0x04000000, read|execute)` on the flash that holds
the UEFI variables (`virt.flash1`). That flash is a ROM device: while the
guest programs it, it is outside ROMD mode and `hvf_set_phys_mem` has unmapped
it. `snapshot-save` and `migrate` start dirty logging, QEMU calls `log_start`
for every range with a RAM block, and the HVF listener protected each of them
whether mapped or not. With `-trace hvf_vm_map -trace hvf_vm_unmap -trace
hvf_vm_protect` the last lines before the abort are two `hvf_vm_unmap` of
`0x04000000` and the `hvf_vm_protect` of the same range.

EDK2 leaves ROMD mode 904 times in the first second after a start (662 of them
between 0.25 s and 0.5 s) and 8 times more at 5.75 s; the flash is unmapped
for 0.17 s in total. Before the patch the engine died in 5 of 40 and in 3 of
40 attempts with the snapshot taken 0.3 s to 0.9 s after the start, and in 0
of 48 with it taken 0.2 s to 2.5 s after the start. A booted guest reaches the
same call whenever it writes a UEFI variable at that moment, and
`VMController.suspendToDisk` reaches it when the vCPUs were stopped between
two flash commands.

The same listener had a second effect: when logging stopped it set every
logged range to read, write and execute, the two flash ranges included. After
any `snapshot-save` the guest's flash writes no longer reached the flash
device: a firmware reboot after a snapshot left the flash 0 times instead of
96 and `efi-vars.fd` stayed unchanged, where the same reboot without a
snapshot changed it.

`qemu-patches/0003` makes `log_start`, `log_stop` and `log_clear` act only on
RAM that `hvf_set_phys_mem` maps writable (`memory_region_is_ram`, not
read-only, not a ROM device). Dirty logging by write protection concerns only
memory the guest writes without an exit; a ROM device is either unmapped or
mapped read-only, and its writes always go through the device. With the patch:
0 of 120 attempts aborted with the snapshot 0.3 s to 0.9 s after the start, 60
of 60 snapshots taken in that window loaded again with the firmware
continuing, the only `hv_vm_protect` calls are those for the RAM at
`0x40000000`, and after a snapshot the firmware reboot leaves ROMD mode 96
times and writes `efi-vars.fd` as without one.
`testFirmwareVariableWritesReachTheFlashFileAfterASnapshot` fails on the
engine without the patch (3 of 3 runs) and passes with it.

- Suspend to disk is limited by the engine's default migration bandwidth
  (`max-bandwidth` 134217728 bytes/s in `query-migrate-parameters`): 32 s to
  38 s for 4.0 GB to 4.7 GB, where `snapshot-save` writes the same memory in 7 s
  to 9 s. `VMController.suspendToDisk` does not raise the limit.
- The control server has no op that suspends a VM; the suspend runs were
  triggered through `VMController.suspendToDisk` in a copy of `alcovectl
  serve`. `alcove_start_vm` of the MCP server does not mention `resumed` or
  `clockSynced`.
- `vm.set_resolution` with a mode the guest does not offer changed the guest
  to another mode (2560x1600 asked, 1920x1200 set; 3840x2400 asked, 3840x2160
  set); the result reports the size that was set.
- A run of `at2-e2e.py` that fails before check 8 leaves the jobs of checks 3
  and 4 listed as `exited` in `vm.exec.list` until the guest tools restart.
- `vm.status` reports `tools: true` while the network link is down, and the
  first guest-tools op after `up: true` can answer `disconnected during <op>`.
  Documented.
- `timeZone` takes the guest's own zone ids; an IANA name is not translated for
  Windows.
- `vm.status` showed `640x480` for a VM that had been restarted until a frame
  was read again; `width` and `height` are those of the latest frame read
  through the server.
- Two processes that own one VM directory, a clone created by another process
  between a template's epoch bump and its engine start, and a template bundle
  moved on disk are limits stated in `docs/control-protocol.md`.
- Not exercised: Linux guests (the `sh` recipes of `vm.set_environment` and
  `vm.doctor`), the Alcove app as the owner of the control socket, a VM with
  more than one disk, `vm.snapshot.restore` and the resume of a suspended
  session with guest tools that cannot run commands (`clockSynced: false`), a
  paused VM.
